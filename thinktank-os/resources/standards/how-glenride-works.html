How Glenride Works

Glenride is an independent, AI-native think tank. We research supply chain vulnerabilities, pharmaceutical data, and enterprise software systems — and we publish what we find.

This page exists because manufactured credibility is now cheap. Fake journalists, fake think tanks, AI-written papers with nobody behind them. So here's exactly how we operate, in plain language. Hold us to it.

How we use AI

We use AI the way a newsroom uses research assistants: to scan, sort, draft, and organize. Every word we publish is reviewed, verified, and signed off by a human before it goes out.

AI prepares. Humans decide. That's not a slogan — it's the process. Nothing here publishes on autopilot.

Sources

We link our sources. When we cite a number, a study, or a claim, you can follow it back to where it came from. Primary sources wherever they exist.

Verification

If we can't verify it, we say so — in the piece, not in a footnote. Estimates are labeled as estimates. Drafts are labeled as drafts. Unconfirmed details are marked as unconfirmed.

We'd rather tell you what we don't know than let you assume we checked.

Corrections

We get things wrong sometimes. When we do, we correct it publicly and date the correction. The original error stays visible with a note — we don't quietly rewrite our mistakes out of existence.

A think tank that never corrects anything either never says anything risky or never checks its work. We're not interested in being either.

What we won't do

  • Publish under fake names or invented personas.
  • Invent statistics, testimonials, or credentials.
  • Present AI-generated content as human work without saying so.
  • Quietly edit published pieces to hide errors.
  • Let sponsors shape conclusions. (Sponsor the findings. Never the conclusions.)

The short version

In an era of manufactured credibility, we show our work. Every claim has a trail. Every mistake has a correction. Every word has a human behind it.

That's what you're getting when you read Glenride: research you can check, from people who'll admit when they're wrong.

— D'Vaughn House, Founder
