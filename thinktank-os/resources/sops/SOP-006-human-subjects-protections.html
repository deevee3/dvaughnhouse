SOP-006 · Human Subjects Protections

Status: DRAFT v0.1 · 2026-10-10 · Not binding until approved Owner: Lead Director · Approver: President

Purpose

The rights, safety, and well-being of research participants come before every other interest — before the science, the timeline, and the budget. This SOP is the floor. No Glenride study involving people begins, continues, or publishes without meeting it.

Scope

Any Glenride work involving human participants, identifiable human data, or human biospecimens — including secondary-data studies where re-identification is possible. When in doubt, it applies.

Procedure

  1. Foundations. Every person on human-subjects work must understand the three Belmont principles and apply them:
  2. Respect for persons — participants are autonomous agents; those with diminished autonomy get extra protection.
  3. Beneficence — maximize benefit, minimize harm. "Do no harm" is the minimum, not the goal.

  4. Justice — the burdens and benefits of research are distributed fairly. No population carries the risk while another takes the benefit.

  5. Independent ethics review — mandatory. Glenride holds no IRB of its own. Before any human-subjects work begins, the study must have written approval from a contracted external Institutional Review Board (IRB) / ethics committee. No approval, no work — no exceptions, no "we'll file it next week." Continuing review happens on the IRB's schedule.

  6. Informed consent. Consent is a process, not a signature. Every consent process covers, in language the participant can understand:

  7. What the study is for and what participation involves
  8. Reasonably foreseeable risks and discomforts
  9. Potential benefits (to the participant and to others)
  10. Alternatives to participation
  11. How confidentiality is protected and its limits
  12. That participation is voluntary and withdrawal is allowed at any time without penalty

  13. Who to contact with questions, concerns, or injuries Consent is documented in writing unless the IRB waives documentation. Coercion or undue influence invalidates consent.

  14. Vulnerable populations. Research involving children, prisoners, pregnant participants, or cognitively impaired individuals triggers additional regulatory protections (the Common Rule's Subparts B, C, and D). Flag these populations at proposal stage (see SOP-001) — they change the review path.

  15. Confidentiality. Identifiable participant data is access-controlled, de-identified where the science allows, and never disclosed outside the approved protocol. A breach follows the incident path in the data-security SOP.

  16. Stop rule. Any team member who believes participant welfare is at risk stops the work and escalates to the Lead Director and the IRB immediately. No retaliation for raising a concern — ever.

Records

  • IRB application, approval letters, and continuing-review records — kept by Operations.
  • Signed consent forms (or IRB-approved waiver documentation), stored separately from study data.
  • Protocol deviations affecting participants, reported to the IRB per its requirements.

Review

Annually, or after any participant complaint, adverse event, or IRB finding.

Basis

The Belmont Report (1979); the Federal Policy for the Protection of Human Subjects ("Common Rule," 45 CFR 46, 2018 revision); the Declaration of Helsinki; ICH E6(R3) Good Clinical Practice.
