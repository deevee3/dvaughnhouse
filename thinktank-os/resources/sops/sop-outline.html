Glenride Research — SOP Outline

Subset of the Glenride operating structure. An outline of the standard operating procedures the research company should have, drawn from best practices (NIH research integrity guidance, FAIR data principles, standard publication ethics, ethics-committee review elements). Each SOP below needs an owner, a version number, and a Presidential approval before it counts. This outline is the table of contents — the full SOPs get written one at a time.

How to read this

  • SOP number is permanent. When an SOP is revised, the version changes; the number doesn't.
  • Owner is the role in the org chain accountable for it (President → Lead Director → Department Directors → Managers → Teams).
  • Best-practice basis names where the standard comes from, so nobody has to take it on faith.

A. Study lifecycle

SOP-01 — Study proposal & approval - Purpose: every study enters through the same door — written proposal, reviewed, approved before a dollar is spent. - Basis: standard research governance; ties directly to the annual budget cycle (studies proposed in the planning year). - Owner: Department Director (Research); approval: President.

SOP-02 — Study design standards - Purpose: minimum bar for a credible design — defined question, appropriate method, sample/data rationale, analysis plan written before data is touched. - Basis: scientific review-committee elements (design, predictable limitations, competence of staff, adequacy of resources). - Owner: Department Director (Research).

SOP-03 — Ethics & data-ethics review - Purpose: every study gets an ethics pass — even secondary-data work. Human data, re-identification risk, and downstream harm get explicit review. - Basis: ethics-committee review elements; NSF-backed ethical data research guidelines (data are people; privacy isn't binary; guard against re-identification; debate the hard choices). - Owner: Lead Director; external ethics board contracted when human-subjects work is involved (Glenride holds no IRB of its own until scale justifies one).

B. Data management

SOP-04 — Data management plans (DMPs) - Purpose: every study writes a DMP before collection — what data, where it lives, who can touch it, how long it's kept. - Basis: FAIR principles — data findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable; planned from the outset, not retrofitted. - Owner: study Manager.

SOP-05 — Data collection & quality control - Purpose: collection methods documented, quality checks defined in advance, missing-data handling specified. - Basis: standard data-integrity practice; post-collection screening methods declared upfront. - Owner: study Manager.

SOP-06 — Data security, privacy & de-identification - Purpose: who can access what, how data is de-identified, how breaches are handled and reported. - Basis: privacy-beyond-binary practice; systems designed for auditability. - Owner: Department Director (Research), with Operations for infrastructure.

SOP-07 — Data retention & archiving - Purpose: how long data lives, where archives sit, what gets destroyed and when, with a record of destruction. - Basis: records-management best practice; funder/journal retention expectations. - Owner: Operations.

SOP-08 — Data sharing & licensing - Purpose: what Glenride shares openly, what it licenses commercially, and the terms for each — decided before publication, not after. - Basis: National Academies data-sharing principles (share what supports the claims; use public repositories; no exclusive commercial lockups on shared materials). - Owner: President (commercial terms); Department Director (scientific terms).

C. Research integrity

SOP-09 — Research records & notebooks - Purpose: every experiment, analysis, and decision is recorded contemporaneously in a system with an audit trail. Corrections are tracked — never silently deleted. Failed runs and outliers stay in the record. - Basis: NIH research-integrity practice (electronic notebooks with audit trails; "could an educated researcher repeat this from your record?"). - Owner: every team member; Managers verify.

SOP-10 — Authorship & contribution - Purpose: who counts as an author (significant contribution to conception, design, execution, or interpretation), who goes in acknowledgements, and the order decided before writing starts. - Basis: standard publication-ethics authorship criteria. - Owner: Department Director (Research).

SOP-11 — Originality & plagiarism - Purpose: all work original; others' work cited or quoted; no duplicate publication of the same research; methods sections reused from prior work get cited. - Basis: standard publication ethics (originality, no redundant publication, acknowledgement of sources). - Owner: Department Director (Research).

SOP-12 — Conflicts of interest - Purpose: financial and personal interests disclosed at study start and at publication; commercial licensing interests declared when they touch the research question. - Basis: standard research-governance disclosure practice. Independence means the money never shapes the finding. - Owner: President.

SOP-13 — Misconduct reporting & investigation - Purpose: a protected channel to report suspected fabrication, falsification, or plagiarism; a defined investigation process; no retaliation. - Basis: federal research-misconduct framework (allegation → inquiry → investigation → adjudication). - Owner: President, with external counsel engaged for investigations.

D. Publication & dissemination

SOP-14 — Internal review before publication - Purpose: no finding leaves Glenride without internal review — methods checked, claims matched to evidence, limitations stated. - Basis: the institute's credibility is the product; review is how it's manufactured. - Owner: Department Director (Research); President signs off on high-visibility releases.

SOP-15 — Publication ethics - Purpose: accurate reporting, no duplicate submission, full methods for reproducibility, data underlying the claims available. - Basis: standard publication-ethics reporting standards. - Owner: Department Director (Research).

SOP-16 — Public communication - Purpose: how findings are communicated outside journals — preprints, press, social. No hype beyond the evidence; uncertainty stated plainly. - Basis: responsible science-communication practice. - Owner: Publications & Story (department), with Research sign-off on technical claims.

E. Study operations

SOP-17 — Study execution & monitoring - Purpose: studies run to the approved plan; deviations documented and approved; progress reported on a fixed cadence. - Basis: Good Clinical Practice monitoring concepts, adapted to data studies. - Owner: study Manager; Department Director reviews.

SOP-18 — Vendor & data-license management - Purpose: data licenses and vendors approved before purchase; terms reviewed; renewals tracked. (Data licenses are a planned budget line — see the annual cycle.) - Basis: standard procurement control. - Owner: Department Director (Research); Operations for contracting.

SOP-19 — Study budget tracking - Purpose: spend tracked per study against the allocated budget as studies complete; variances explained; leftovers flagged for reallocation. - Basis: the institute's own budget cycle (Execute → Reallocate). - Owner: study Manager; Lead Director reallocates.

F. People & training

SOP-20 — Onboarding & training records - Purpose: every team member trained on the SOPs that touch their work; training recorded; retraining on SOP revision. - Basis: standard quality-system practice — an SOP nobody was trained on doesn't exist. - Owner: Managers.

SOP-21 — Roles, responsibilities & delegation - Purpose: the org chain in writing — who can approve what, who can spend what, what needs Presidential sign-off. - Basis: the operating structure concept. - Owner: President.

G. Documents & the SOPs themselves

SOP-22 — Document control - Purpose: every SOP versioned, approved, and distributed from one controlled set. Superseded versions archived, never deleted. Everybody works from the current version. - Basis: ISO-9001-style document control, scaled to an institute. - Owner: Operations.

SOP-23 — SOP review cycle - Purpose: every SOP reviewed on a fixed schedule (annual) and after any incident or audit finding. The system improves or it rots. - Basis: continuous-improvement practice. - Owner: Lead Director.

H. Research fundamentals

SOP-005 — Data integrity: ALCOA+ principles - Purpose: the minimum bar for trustworthy data — attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, available. Corrections never obscure the original. - Basis: FDA data-integrity guidance; ICH E6(R3). - Owner: every team member; Managers verify.

SOP-006 — Human subjects protections - Purpose: participant rights, safety, and well-being before every other interest. External IRB approval mandatory — Glenride holds no IRB of its own. Informed consent as a process, not a signature. - Basis: the Belmont Report; the Common Rule (45 CFR 46); the Declaration of Helsinki; ICH E6(R3). - Owner: Lead Director; external ethics board contracted for review.

SOP-007 — Clinical trial phases & fundamentals - Purpose: one shared language for trial stages (Phase 0–IV), regulatory gates (IND/NDA/BLA), and design vocabulary (randomization, blinding, control, endpoints). - Basis: FDA investigational-drug regulations (21 CFR Part 312); ICH E6(R3); ICH E8(R1). - Owner: Department Director (Research).

SOP-008 — Research training requirements - Purpose: nobody touches regulated work untrained. CITI (human subjects) and GCP (ICH E6(R3)) with three-year refreshers; training log with 60-day expiry flags. - Basis: CITI Program curriculum structure; ICH E6(R3) training expectations. - Owner: Managers.

SOP-009 — International standards for data harmonization - Purpose: datasets built to ICH and CDISC standards (CDASH → SDTM → ADaM) from design stage, with MedDRA/WHODrug coding — interoperable, auditable, submission-grade. - Basis: ICH E6(R3), E8(R1), E9; CDISC standards; FAIR principles. - Owner: Department Director (Research).

SOP-010 — The Nuremberg Code - Purpose: the origin point of modern research ethics — the Doctors' Trial, the ten points in operational form, and the lineage (Helsinki → Belmont → Common Rule → ICH-GCP). History is why the rules are non-negotiable. - Basis: United States v. Karl Brandt et al. (1947); Declaration of Helsinki; the Belmont Report. - Owner: Department Director (Research).

Writing order (suggested)

Start where the risk is: SOP-01, SOP-09, SOP-12, SOP-19 — how studies get approved, how records are kept, how conflicts are disclosed, how money is tracked. Then data management (SOP-04 through SOP-08), then the rest. Four strong SOPs beat twenty-three drafts.

End of outline. Each SOP gets written as its own document, owned, versioned, and approved — that's when it becomes real.
