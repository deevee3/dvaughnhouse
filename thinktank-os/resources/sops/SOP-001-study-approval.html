SOP-001 · Study Approval

Status: DRAFT v0.1 · 2026-10-08 · Not binding until approved Owner: Lead Director · Approver: President

Purpose

No study starts without written approval. This is the gate that keeps unfunded, off-mission, or ethically unexamined work from consuming budget — and the receipt that proves a study was authorized.

Scope

Every research study, trial, data-licensing engagement, and published analysis run under the Glenride name, in any of the seven departments.

Procedure

  1. Proposal. The requesting Manager submits a one-page study proposal to their Department Director:
  2. Research question in one sentence
  3. Design and method (how the question gets answered)
  4. Data sources required, including any licenses to buy and their cost
  5. Budget estimate, tied to the department's allocation
  6. Ethics and data considerations (subjects, privacy, consent, dual use)
  7. Proposed team and each member's role

  8. Reference to the authorship plan (see the authorship SOP when written; until then, name who writes)

  9. Director review. The Department Director checks fit with the department mandate and the year's plan, then forwards to the Lead Director with a written recommendation or return with notes.

  10. Budget gate. The Lead Director checks the proposal against the five-stage budget cycle: studies run on what was planned the year prior. A study that was not planned needs the exception path (step 5).

  11. Presidential approval. The President approves or declines in writing. Approval enters the study in the study register with a unique study ID and "approved" status. Declines go back with reasons. No study begins before this step.

  12. Exception path (unplanned study). The request goes to the President with a written rationale for why it cannot wait for the next planning cycle. Approval is the same as step 4; the register marks it "exception."

Records

  • The study register: proposal, Director recommendation, Lead Director budget check, President decision, study ID, status — kept by Operations.
  • Appeals: none informal — a declined proposal may be resubmitted once with material changes.

Review

Annually, or after the first three studies close, whichever comes first — then the procedure gets its first revision from real use.
